# research-bpm
## Findings
- [high] librosa's default pipeline: onset strength is a mel spectrogram converted to dB, then a lag-1 difference, half-wave rectified and averaged over bands (n_fft 2048, hop 512 at 22.05 kHz, about 23 ms). Tempo is the argmax of log1p(1e6*tempogram) plus a log-normal prior: logprior = -0.5*((log2(bpm)-log2(120))/1.0)^2, with an autocorrelation window ac_size=8 s (384 frames) and max_tempo 320. beat_track is Ellis-style dynamic programming: tightness=100, transition cost -tightness*(log(interval)-log(period))^2, predecessor search from -2P to -P/2, onset envelope normalized by its std.
- [high] Ellis 2007 (JNMR 36(1)): global tempo comes from the autocorrelation of onset strength, weighted by a perceptual window peaking at 120 BPM with a width of 1.4 octaves. The DP then picks beats that balance onset strength against a log-interval penalty. It assumes a roughly constant tempo and needs a tempo estimate as input (FMP notes the result depends on the choice of lambda).
- [high] Mixxx's default analyzer is QM-DSP. Onset function is complex spectral difference (DF_COMPLEXSD), step 0.01161 s (512 samples at 44.1 kHz), window nextPow2(sr/50) (1024 at 44.1 kHz). TempoTrackV2 settings: 512-frame (~6 s) windows with a 128-frame (~1.5 s) hop; Rayleigh weighting rayparam=(60*44100/512)/120, about 43 (a 120 BPM prior); a 4-harmonic comb over the ACF; Viterbi tempo path with sigma=8; beat DP with tightness 4 and alpha 0.9. Mixxx then post-processes the beats in BeatUtils: kMaxSecsPhaseError=25 ms, kMaxSecsPhaseErrorSum=100 ms, kMaxOutliersCount=1, kMinRegionBeatCount=16. It keeps the longest constant region and snaps BPM to an integer, then 1/2 (below 85), 2/3 (above 127), 1/3, then 1/12.
- [high] Mixxx is still fixing grid phase and offset bugs in 2025-26. 2.6 adds 'QueenMaryBeats: fix offset issue' (#15076: the first full 6 s window was treated as position 1, so tempo changes were detected up to 3 s early) and a ~10% speedup (#15127). Issue #15848 (Mixxx 2.5.3) reports the BPM is right but the grid sits on the upbeat, i.e. a half-beat phase error. The Mixxx manual warns that even a 0.02 BPM error drifts over long tracks, and Mixxx offers 'Assume constant tempo' and 'Enable fast analysis' (first minute only).
- [high] aubio tempo: the 'default' mode uses the specflux onset function with buffer 1024 and hop 512. It uses Davies & Plumbley beat tracking: a window of nextPow2(5.8 s) with step = window/4, a Rayleigh prior at 120 BPM, a 4-element comb filterbank, and confidence = peak magnitude / ACF sum. BTrack is causal and real-time: complex spectral difference (half-wave rectified), hop 512 and frame 1024, tempo forced into 80-160 by octave folding, Rayleigh 43, tightness 5, alpha 0.9, and a 41-state tempo transition matrix.
- [high] Essentia PercivalBpmEstimator (Percival & Tzanetakis, IEEE TASLP 2014) defaults: frame 1024, hop 128, OSS frame 2048, OSS hop 128, BPM range 50-210. Processing: log(1+1000*|X|) compression, half-wave-rectified L1 flux, a 15-tap FIR low-pass, generalized autocorrelation with exponent 0.5, and the top 10 peaks. Each peak is scored with pulse trains (4 beats, plus 0.5-weighted 2x and 1.5x pulses) on max and variance. A hard-coded SVM picks x2, x1 or x0.5. RhythmExtractor2013 needs 44.1 kHz input and defaults to 40-208 BPM. Its 'multifeature' method reports confidence on 0-5.32 (above 3.5 is excellent); 'degara' always reports 0.
- [medium] Essentia.js is AGPLv3 (also sold under a commercial license) and is about 2.5 MB of WASM plus JS. The TISMIR paper benchmarks a Redmi Note 7 Pro at 0.46-3.48 s per 30 s clip for typical algorithms, with Chrome the slowest browser. There are no published rhythm-specific mobile numbers. It is a heavy, licence-encumbered dependency for one feature.
- [high,critical] Octave errors dominate tempo estimation. In the TISMIR 2020 overview, Percival's Acc1 drops to zero above about 150 BPM. On GiantSteps (EDM), the best Acc1 was only 58.9% (64.8% after the annotations were corrected), against 86.4% to 94.0% for Acc2. Even state-of-the-art systems therefore get the metrical level wrong on a large share of EDM tracks, so x2 and /2 buttons plus a range setting are mandatory.
- [high,critical] web-audio-beat-detector (chrisguttandin, based on Joe Sullivan's 2014 article) works as follows. An OfflineAudioContext applies a 240 Hz lowpass, and only channel 0 is used. Peaks are threshold crossings of the raw waveform, with the threshold lowered from 0.95*max to 0.3*max until at least 30 peaks are found, skipping 0.25 s after each peak. Intervals to the next 10 peaks are folded into 90-180 BPM and grouped within ±0.5 BPM. guess() returns bpm=Math.round(tempo) and offset = first peak mod beat. The README itself only claims 'surprisingly good results especially for electronic music'. It has no sub-BPM precision for a grid and no phase averaged over the track, and the offset comes from a single loud peak. Fine as a rough hint, unsuitable for DJ sync.
- [high] Scheirer 1998 (JASA 103:588-601) uses 6 bandpass bands, an envelope per band, and a bank of parallel comb-filter resonators. QM-DSP, aubio and BTrack use descendants of this (a comb filterbank over the ACF with a Rayleigh prior). Fourier-tempogram / PLP (Grosche & Müller) takes beat phase from the angle of the Fourier coefficient at the beat frequency.
- [high,critical] Grid drift = (ΔBPM/BPM) * elapsed time. Keeping drift under 10 ms over 6 min at 128 BPM needs ΔBPM ≤ 0.0036. Integer-rounded BPM (web-audio-beat-detector) on a 127.5 track is off by 0.39%, which drifts half a beat per minute. Keep BPM as an unrounded float64 internally and round only for display.
- [high,critical] Prototype results (Chrome 152 desktop, synthetic signals; real music not yet tested). BPM was exact (|err| ≤ 0.0008) on all 16 cases: clicks, house with off-beat open hats, house with off-beat bass at 48 kHz, 126.37 with 4 ms and 10 ms humanize, 5.3 s silence plus a 30 s drumless pad intro, a 60 s breakdown at 127.5, hip-hop at 80/84/88/92 with swing 0.5-0.66, DnB at 172/174/176, half-time dubstep at 140, and -20 dB noise. Integer and half-BPM snapping worked. After a constant +2.6 ms calibration, the folded-phase first beat was within ±0.4 ms, or 1.6-4.1 ms when tonal pad onsets were present. Noise-only and pad-only gave confidence 0. Tempo wobble of 0.5% and 2%, and a 120→124 ramp, were flagged variable (residual RMS 33-210 ms against 1-4 ms for constant tracks).
- [high,critical] Measured pitfall: beat phase from the Fourier coefficient angle (the PLP-style phase) is biased by syncopation. Errors were -72 to -103 ms on the hip-hop patterns (kicks on 1.75 and 2.5), +37 ms on half-time dubstep, and -18 ms on DnB with swing. Folding the onset signal modulo the final period (synchronous averaging, 128 bins per beat) and taking the peak fixes this to under 0.5 ms. Use the Fourier phase only to track phase changes window to window (tempo refinement and drift), never for the absolute grid anchor.
- [medium,critical] Measured pitfall: deciding the octave from log-compressed flux fails because log compression equalizes hits. A kick's tiny mid-band click scored as high as the snare, which picked 87 instead of 174 for DnB and 168 instead of 84 for hip-hop. Folding the LINEAR energy flux of the band above 2.5 kHz on the candidate fast grid separates them cleanly: rho (opposite slot / strongest slot over a 4-fast-beat cycle) was 0.99-1.0 for DnB and 0.07 for hip-hop. The rule: the backbeat lands on 2 and 4, so beat period = snare period / 2. The rho > 0.5 threshold was tuned on synthetic data only.
- [medium] Performance: the whole pipeline took 96 ms for an 8-minute 48 kHz mono track on desktop Chrome 152, of which the onset stage (decimation plus 5 biquads plus energy) was 77 ms, ACF 9 ms, zoom 8 ms, phase and fold about 1 ms. A 3-minute 44.1 kHz track took 25-70 ms. No FFT is needed. Phones were not measured; assuming they are 4-8x slower, 0.4-0.8 s for 8 minutes, well inside the 2 s budget.
- [high,critical] BaseAudioContext (and therefore OfflineAudioContext and decodeAudioData) and AudioBuffer are [Exposed=Window] in the Web Audio spec, so decoding cannot happen inside a Worker. decodeAudioData resamples to the context's sample rate, which is often 48 kHz on phones. Decode on the main thread, then send PCM to the worker.
- [medium] How commercial DJ apps handle analysis errors. Traktor: x2 and /2 buttons in the GRID tab, tapping guides auto-detection, grid lock, default range 78-155 (the range figure comes from a third-party summary). Serato: a BPM Range setting (58-115, 68-135, 78-155, 88-175, 98-195) to avoid half/double results. rekordbox: 'Normal' mode with a BPM range for steady tracks, 'Dynamic' mode for live or drifting tempo, plus GRID-panel TAP, halve/double, set first beat and nudge.
## Recommendations
- ARCHITECTURE: on the main thread, after decodeAudioData, build a decimated mono copy: D=round(sr/11025), which is 4 for both 44.1 and 48 kHz; m[i] = mean of D samples of (L+R)/2. Do it in chunks of about 1 s of audio per task so the UI (scratching) does not freeze. Transfer that Float32Array to the analysis Worker (8 min at 48 kHz becomes 23 MB instead of 184 MB for stereo float). The worker returns {bpm, firstBeat, confidence, variableTempo, alternatives[], beatMap?}.
- STAGE 1, ONSET STRENGTH (no FFT): fsd = 11025 or 12000 Hz. Bands: low = 2 cascaded RBJ lowpass at 150 Hz, Q 0.7071; mid = HP 200 then LP 2500; high = HP 2500. Sum squared output per hop of H=64 samples (5.8/5.3 ms, fps 172.3/187.5). Energy E[n] = s[n]+s[n-1] (2-hop window). Log-compress per band with L = log1p(100*E/mean(E_band)), where mean is over the whole track. Flux D[n] = max(0, L[n]-L[n-2]). Use ossT = low + mid + 0.5*high for tempo and ossP = low + mid for phase (no hats, to avoid locking to off-beats). Subtract a centred moving average of ±0.25 s and half-wave rectify.
- STAGE 2, TEMPO CANDIDATES: sum ossT in pairs (fps2 ≈ 86-94 Hz) and remove the mean. Compute a direct global ACF for lags 0..ceil(4*fps2) (60 BPM x4), normalized by A[0]; about 17M multiply-adds for 8 min. Comb score C(τ) = A(τ)+A(2τ)+A(4τ) with linearly interpolated fractional lags, τ in 0.05-frame steps covering 60-200 BPM. Multiply by the prior exp(-0.5*(log2(bpm/120)/1.0)^2). Take the top 6 local maxima with parabolic interpolation and keep them for the UI's 'alternatives'. Fold the best one into [78,156).
- STAGE 3a, FINE TEMPO: whole-track zoomed DFT power Σ_{h=1,2}|Σ x[n]e^{-i2π h f n/fps}|² on ossP decimated by 2. Search ±1% in 49 steps (the prototype used ±0.6%), then ±0.06% in 13 steps, with parabolic interpolation. Use a phasor recursion (rotate cos/sin with renormalisation every 1024 samples) instead of Math.sin per sample.
- STAGE 3b, PHASE REGRESSION AND DRIFT: Hann windows of 8 s with a 4 s hop. For each window compute z = Σ w·ossP[n]·e^{-i2πfn/fps}, phase β = -arg(z)/2π in beats, coherence r = |z|/Σw·ossP. A window is confident if r > 0.15 and |z| > 0.1*max|z|. Unwrap outward from the strongest window, always relative to the last confident window. Weighted least squares β = a + s·t (weights |z|), with 2-3 passes rejecting residuals over 30 ms. Then f' = f - s. Run Stage 3b twice. Flag variable tempo if the residual RMS exceeds 12 ms or the maximum exceeds 40 ms.
- STAGE 4, OCTAVE (only when the folded bpm is below 90 and 2*bpm ≤ 180): fold the LINEAR energy flux of the high band (>2.5 kHz) on the fast (2x) grid, using the folded phase, into 4 fast-beat slots (only frames within ±0.12 beat of a slot). rho = v[(argmax+2)%4] / v[argmax]. If rho > 0.5, report 2x (DnB 172-176); otherwise report the slow tempo (hip-hop 80-90). The effective reported range is therefore [78,180). Half-time dubstep and trap at 140 already fall inside [78,156).
- STAGE 5, SNAP: skip if variable or if the confident span is under 20 s. Otherwise tol = bpm*0.008/span_seconds; snap to round(bpm) if within tol, else to round(2*bpm)/2. Do NOT snap to thirds or twelfths in v1. Keep the unsnapped float otherwise; live and non-DAW tracks such as 126.37 must stay unsnapped.
- STAGE 6, GRID ANCHOR: fold ossP modulo the final period into 128 bins per beat with linear splatting, smooth with a circular Gaussian (σ=1.5 bins), take the argmax plus a parabolic offset. Then firstBeat = (frac/f - latencyBias) mod P, where latencyBias = 2.6 ms for these exact settings (H=64, 2-hop energy, lag-2 flux). Re-calibrate this bias with the click test whenever H, the window or the lag changes. Never use the Fourier angle as the anchor.
- CONFIDENCE: contrast = (foldPeak - foldMedian)/foldPeak. conf = clamp((contrast-0.3)/0.5, 0, 1) * min(1, coverage/0.5) * (variable ? 0.5 : 1), where coverage is the fraction of confident windows. If conf < 0.3, show 'BPM ?' and prompt the user to tap. Also expose the octave rho and the alternatives so the UI can highlight the x2 and /2 buttons when rho is in 0.3-0.7.
- VARIABLE-TEMPO TRACKS: for v1, show a 'variable tempo' badge, use the average BPM, and make SYNC tempo-only (no phase lock), or build a beat map from the per-window phases. To build it, correct each window's Fourier phase by the global offset (foldPhase - FourierPhase), which is pattern-dependent but constant within a track, interpolate between windows, and emit beat times. Ellis DP (tightness about 100) is an alternative if the engine supports beat maps. This matches rekordbox's Normal/Dynamic split.
- UI FIXES (large touch targets): x2 and /2 buttons; TAP (4 or more taps, linear regression on tap times gives BPM and phase, then re-run Stage 3a constrained to ±2% of the tap BPM and Stage 6 for a precise grid); grid nudge ±1 ms / ±10 ms buttons or drag on the waveform; 'beat 1 here' (t0 = playhead mod P); BPM fine ±0.01; range preset (78-155 default, 88-175 for DnB-heavy, 58-115 for slow); a grid lock icon. Persist per-track edits in IndexedDB keyed by name+size+duration, and save the analysis result too so re-loading skips work.
- UNIT TESTS (browser-only, no Node): use the deterministic synth (mulberry32 PRNG; one-shots: kick = 45+110·e^{-t/25ms} Hz sine sweep with 180 ms decay plus a 2 ms click; snare = 190 Hz sine plus noise band-passed at 2 kHz; clap = 3 bursts of noise band-passed at 1.2 kHz; hats = noise high-passed at 7 kHz, 15 ms closed / 120 ms open; bass = 55 Hz sine on off-beats). Assert: |ΔBPM| ≤ 0.005 on constant tracks; |phase| ≤ 3 ms after calibration; correct octave for hip-hop 80/84/88/92 (swing 0.5-0.66), DnB 172/174/176 and dubstep 140; house with off-beat open hats and off-beat bass locks to the kick; 127.5 snaps to 127.5 and 126.37 is not snapped; 5.3 s silence plus a 30 s pad intro and a 60 s breakdown still give the right grid; ±0.5% wobble and a 120→124 ramp set variableTempo, while 10 ms humanize does not; noise-only and pad-only give conf 0; the same pattern at 44.1 and 48 kHz agrees; an 8 min 48 kHz track stays under the time budget. Then add 20-50 real tracks from the user's own library with hand-checked BPM and grid as a regression set to tune rho, contrast and the drift thresholds.
- LIBRARY CHOICE: implement the ~300 lines in-house (prototype: analyzer.js). Do not ship web-audio-beat-detector (integer BPM, single-peak offset) or essentia.js (AGPLv3, ~2.5 MB WASM, 44.1 kHz-only RhythmExtractor2013) for the core grid. essentia.js could serve as an offline reference when building test labels.
## Gotchas
- Fourier/PLP phase is biased by syncopation: measured -100 ms on hip-hop and +37 ms on dubstep. Anchor the grid by folding the onset signal, not by the coefficient angle.
- Log-compressed flux evens out loud and quiet hits. That is good for finding periodicity and bad for loudness judgements: the octave/backbeat test must use LINEAR energy flux from the >2.5 kHz band. With log flux, the kick click outvoted the snare and both DnB and hip-hop octaves came out wrong.
- Including hats or the high band in the phase onset signal makes house tracks with loud off-beat open hats lock to the off-beat, which is the classic half-beat grid error (compare Mixxx issue #15848). Use low+mid only for phase.
- Leading silence and quiet intros: log(E+eps) turns noise-floor fluctuations into large flux spikes. Use log1p(γ·E/meanE_band) so near-silence maps to about 0, and down-weight low-coherence windows. Extrapolate the grid backwards through drumless intros and report firstBeat as t0 mod P.
- Never round BPM for sync or the grid. 0.02 BPM already drifts audibly on a long track, and drift = ΔBPM/BPM × time. Keep a float64 and display to 0.1.
- The decoded buffer is resampled to the AudioContext rate (often 48 kHz on phones). Store grid positions in seconds, not sample indices, and compute them from the exact decoded buffer that is played. Different MP3 decoders can trim encoder delay and padding differently (tens of ms; not verified per browser here), so do not reuse grid offsets computed by another decoder or app without a check.
- decodeAudioData and OfflineAudioContext are Window-only. Decode on the main thread, decimate there in chunks, and transfer only a 12 kHz mono buffer to the worker. An 8-minute stereo 48 kHz float AudioBuffer is about 184 MB; two decks plus copies can trigger iOS tab kills.
- Half-time and double-time ambiguity cannot be removed completely. Hip-hop at 70-77 BPM will be reported as 140-154 with the 78-155 range (Traktor and Serato behave the same), and trap or dubstep is genuinely ambiguous (70 vs 140). Always offer x2 and /2 and remember the user's correction per track.
- 3:2 and 2:3 errors (shuffle or triplet feel) are not auto-corrected. Expose bpm*2/3 and bpm*3/2 among the alternatives, and treat TAP as the fallback.
- The octave rho threshold (0.5), the confidence mapping (contrast 0.3-0.8) and the drift thresholds (12 ms RMS / 40 ms max) were tuned only on synthetic signals. Real mixes with sidechained pads, vocal sibilance in the >2.5 kHz band, or snare rolls may shift them, so a real-track regression set is required.
- Tracks shorter than about 20 s are not snapped and get lower precision. Tracks with abrupt tempo changes (DJ edits, medleys) are flagged variable and will not phase-sync correctly with a single constant grid.
- The +2.6 ms latency bias depends on the hop, window and lag choices. Re-measure it with the click-track test whenever any analysis parameter changes.
- In this environment the browser pane cannot script file:// pages and there is no Node or Python. Serve test pages with the PowerShell HttpListener script (serve.ps1) or the app's own dev server.

```js
// PSEUDOCODE: worker entry. mono = decimated mono buffer (fsd ≈ 11-12 kHz)
function analyze(mono, fsd){
  const H=64, fps=fsd/H;
  const [eLow,eMid,eHigh] = bandEnergies(mono, fsd, H);          // LP150x2 | HP200->LP2500 | HP2500
  const lf = e => { const E=e[n]+e[n-1]; L=log1p(100*E/mean(E)); return max(0,L[n]-L[n-2]); };
  const ossT = localMeanSubHWR(lf(eLow)+lf(eMid)+0.5*lf(eHigh), 0.25*fps);
  const ossP = localMeanSubHWR(lf(eLow)+lf(eMid),               0.25*fps);
  // 2. candidates
  const x = pairSum(ossT), A = acf(x, 4*fps/2) / A0;
  const C = tau => A(tau)+A(2*tau)+A(4*tau);                     // fractional, linear interp
  const cands = localMaxima(tau => max(0,C(tau))*exp(-0.5*(log2(bpm(tau)/120)/1.0)**2), 60..200 BPM);
  let bpm = foldInto(cands[0].bpm, 78, 156);
  // 3a. zoom: maximise Σ_{h=1,2}|DFT_h(f)|² over ±1% then ±0.06%
  bpm = zoom(pairSum(ossP), fps/2, bpm, 0.01, 24); bpm = zoom(..., 0.0006, 6);
  // 3b. windowed phase regression (twice)
  let pf = phaseFit(ossP, fps, bpm); pf = phaseFit(ossP, fps, pf.bpm); // returns bpm', resid, coverage
  // 4. octave, ambiguous zone only
  if (pf.bpm < 90 && 2*pf.bpm <= 180) {
    const g = foldPhase(ossP, fps, 2*pf.bpm);
    const v = slots4(linearFlux(eHigh), fps, 2*pf.bpm, g.t0);    // ±0.12 beat
    if (v[(argmax(v)+2)%4]/max(v) > 0.5) pf = phaseFit(ossP, fps, 2*pf.bpm);
  }
  const variable = pf.residRms > 12 || pf.residMax > 40;          // ms
  if (!variable && pf.span > 20) pf.bpm = snap(pf.bpm, pf.bpm*0.008/pf.span); // integer, then .5
  const fold = foldPhase(ossP, fps, pf.bpm);                        // 128 bins, gauss σ=1.5, parabolic
  const P = 60/pf.bpm, firstBeat = mod(fold.t0 - 0.0026, P);
  const conf = clamp((fold.contrast-0.3)/0.5)*min(1,pf.coverage/0.5)*(variable?0.5:1);
  return { bpm: pf.bpm, firstBeat, confidence: conf, variableTempo: variable,
           alternatives: [pf.bpm/2, pf.bpm*2, pf.bpm*2/3, pf.bpm*3/2] };
}
```

## Open questions
- Not yet validated on real music. Run the analyzer on 20-50 tracks from the user's library, or on GiantSteps tempo / Ballroom if available, to tune the rho threshold (0.5), the confidence mapping and the drift thresholds.
- Phone timings were not measured. Measure the onset stage on a mid-range Android device (Chrome) and an iPhone (Safari) and decide whether the main-thread decimation needs a cheaper path, for example doing it inside the deck AudioWorklet's PCM hand-off.
- Can the playback engine follow a beat map (variable tempo), or only a constant grid? That decides whether live and drifting tracks get phase sync or only tempo sync.
- Downbeat (bar-phase) estimation was not prototyped. A candidate approach is to pick, among the 4 beat offsets, the one with the most linear low-band (kick) energy plus section-change novelty at 16/32-beat boundaries, with a 'beat 1 here' button as the fallback.
- Should the octave test also cover 3:2 confusions automatically, or are alternatives plus TAP enough for v1?
- Do MP3 encoder-delay trimming differences between Chrome and Safari decodeAudioData matter if grids are cached in IndexedDB and the same file is later opened in a different browser? This was not verified because the search budget ran out.
