# research-ux
## Findings
- [high] Most mobile DJ apps still put performance in LANDSCAPE and use portrait for the library and prep, or for a single focused deck. rekordbox for iOS (v4, 2023) says to prepare playlists in portrait, then turn the phone to landscape to mix, scratch and add FX on virtual decks. A reviewer says the landscape layout works best on an iPhone XR-sized screen or larger.
- [high] djay (Algoriddim) on iOS has these views: Starter, Classic (the default; two virtual turntables), Pro (jog wheels, detailed track info, scrolling waveforms), Automix, Looper, 4 Decks and Video. It follows device orientation, and the docs say horizontal gives more space and mimics a standard DJ layout. Starter keeps only the essentials (two decks, crossfader, XY FX pad) and tempo-blends automatically as you move the crossfader.
- [medium] djay has moved toward portrait on phones. Its 2-Deck mode shows taller vertical scrolling waveforms in portrait and longer horizontal waveforms in landscape. Recent iPhone release notes list 'Waveforms in portrait tools (iPhone)' and a refined portrait/landscape video layout; the App Store shows v5.4.3 dated 2025-09-24, and v5.4.3 also added iOS 26 Liquid Glass and iPhone 17/Air layouts. The Digital DJ Tips djay Pro 5 review praises the 'really nice portrait mode' for casual DJing on the go. Exact version numbers differ between sources.
- [high] rekordbox Lite mode for iOS/Android (July 2025, free, for beginners) has a 2-Deck mode and a Single mode with big album art that supports portrait. It uses large on-screen buttons for FX such as echo, shows visual feedback on every tap, and tints the jog wheel and background from the artwork colours. One tap switches to the full rekordbox UI. Full rekordbox mobile offers 2-deck or 4-deck views with vertical or horizontal waveforms ([2Deck Horizontal]/[2Deck Vertical]).
- [medium] Cross DJ (Mixvibes) keeps the phone main screen minimal: big CUE, PLAY and SYNC buttons, a crossfader, and parallel waveforms that can switch to two whole-track overviews. Volume and EQ sit behind a button as big sliders, and FX are two X/Y pads. Decks are colour-coded orange and blue. The reviewer criticised EQ sliders that did not support multi-touch. The review is from about 2014-2016, so it is dated.
- [medium] edjing Mix always shows two decks and a crossfader. FX, loops, samples and the 3-band EQ open in per-deck panels; the marketing says each feature is reachable in under one touch with the main features always on display. The FX list can be reordered, and portrait mode is switched on from a Modes tab. I could not verify exact pixel layouts.
- [medium] VirtualDJ has no native iPhone or Android DJ app. The App Store 'VirtualDJ' listing is macOS-only (2026 b9307), and on mobile there is only VirtualDJ Remote, a skinnable controller for desktop VirtualDJ. It is not a useful reference for a phone layout.
- [high,critical] djay on iOS does headphone pre-cueing without extra hardware only through 'Split Output': master on one stereo channel, cue on the other, using a stereo-to-mono Y splitter cable. Its docs state that pre-cueing is not supported with Bluetooth or AirPlay devices. This affects the user's two-Bluetooth-output requirement: the UX must detect what the device can do and offer a split-mono fallback.
- [high] Target-size guidance: Apple HIG asks for at least a 44x44 pt hit region (60x60 on visionOS). Material asks for at least 48x48 dp with at least 8 dp between targets (about 9 mm). WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.8 (AA) requires 24x24 CSS px or 24 px spacing, and SC 2.5.5 (AAA) requires 44x44 CSS px. With width=device-width, 1 CSS px is about 1 pt on iPhone and about 1 dp on Android, so 48 CSS px meets all of them.
- [high] Relative vs absolute fader response, using TouchOSC's definitions: in absolute mode any touch jumps the control to the finger position. In relative mode the first touch changes nothing and later movement applies relative to it, which avoids value jumps; a 'factor' sets the sensitivity.
- [medium] For knobs on touch screens, vertical drag (up = more) is preferred over circular rotation. Circular drag breaks down near the centre, and the finger hides the knob. About 200 px of travel for the full range is common, with touch-action:none and setPointerCapture. Some phone UIs draw knobs as vertical bar sliders instead.
- [high,critical] How touch-action works: it is NOT inherited. When a gesture starts, the browser intersects the values of the touched element and its ancestors up to the scroll container, and changing touch-action mid-gesture has no effect. pointercancel fires when the browser takes over a pan or zoom, or when there are too many simultaneous pointers. Every draggable control and all its non-scrolling ancestors need touch-action:none, or two-finger use (fader plus platter) can turn into a pinch and cancel both pointers. touch-action has been Baseline since Sept 2019.
- [medium] iOS Safari ignores user-scalable=no and maximum-scale (since iOS 10). Pinch and double-tap zoom have to be blocked with touch-action (none, or pan-x pan-y on scroll areas). iOS also fires non-standard gesturestart/gesturechange events, and calling preventDefault on them is a common extra safeguard.
- [medium] Pull-to-refresh and rubber-banding: set overscroll-behavior:none on BOTH html and body. One test found that Chrome Android honours it on body and Safari 16 on html.
- [medium] Long-press callouts: -webkit-touch-callout:none exists only in iOS Safari. One iOS 26.1 report says it no longer suppresses the long-press menu on text. Android long-press is governed by the contextmenu event and user-select. So controls must contain no selectable text (user-select:none) and should call preventDefault on contextmenu.
- [medium] Haptics: no iOS browser supports the Vibration API (WebKit never shipped it, and there is no flag). Chrome Android's navigator.vibrate returns false until the user has tapped the frame, and a cross-origin iframe must itself have been activated. iOS 18 (Safari 18.0) added a system haptic when an <input type=checkbox switch> toggles; the switch control itself shipped in Safari 17.4 without haptics. Libraries such as web-haptics and use-haptic get a tick by calling label.click() on a hidden switch. iOS treats this as UI feedback, so it is not muted by Low Power Mode, while Android Battery Saver does suppress vibrate.
- [medium,critical] Android gesture navigation treats horizontal swipes that start at the left or right screen edge as Back, which steals slider and seek-bar drags. Native apps can opt out with the Gesture Exclusion API (200 dp limit per edge), but web pages cannot, so horizontal drag controls must start away from the edges. iOS Safari's edge-swipe Back behaves the same way.
- [high] Jog and platter convention: in VINYL mode, touching the top scratches and stops the track as a held record would, and the side or rim does pitch bend. With vinyl off, the whole surface nudges. Mixxx models a touch-scratch as a virtual 33⅓ rpm record (1.8 s of audio per revolution) smoothed by an alpha-beta filter, starting at alpha=1/8 and beta=alpha/32.
- [high] CDJ CUE semantics: when paused, pressing CUE sets the cue point at the current position. Holding CUE previews from the cue point and releasing returns there; pressing PLAY while holding continues playback. When playing, pressing CUE jumps back to the cue point and pauses. djay changed its cue button to match CDJ and controller behaviour, though some users asked for the old behaviour back.
- [high] Accident-prevention conventions: CDJ 'Needle Lock' (on by default) ignores touches on the track-overview strip while the deck is audible and allows them when paused or not audible. Serato 'Lock Playing Deck' and rekordbox 'Eject/Load Lock' block loading or ejecting a track on a playing deck.
- [medium] djay Sync behaviour: there are two sync types, BPM only and BPM plus beat (which locks beatgrids and phase). An active sync lights the button blue, and the beat-sync variant draws a blue box around it. A forum answer says holding Sync reverts the deck to its original BPM. djay's beginner guide also suggests nudging by dragging the waveform left or right and starting with tracks within 2-5 BPM of each other.
- [high] djay Crossfader FX and Tempo Blend: crossfader moves can trigger Echo, Filter, Tremolo, Sweep, Riser or Neural Mix transitions with a set length in bars, and a blue centre line shows the mode is on. 'Sync + Tempo Blend' auto-beatmatches as you crossfade. djay also has one-tap 'instant FX' (NM Echo Out, Vinyl Stop, Loop, Beatmasher) and Automix, which picks transition points automatically.
- [high] Frequency-coloured waveforms: rekordbox 3Band shows low in blue, mid in orange and high in white. rekordbox RGB and Serato show low in red, mid in green and high in blue. Classic Blue shows low in blue and high in white.
- [medium] The effects beginners use most are the filter (the most common, used for transitions), echo (to end a blend or echo out the last beat or vocal) and reverb (in breakdowns).
- [medium] Viewport facts. svh, lvh and dvh are supported in Safari 15.4+, Chrome 108+ and Firefox 101+, and became Baseline Widely Available in June 2025. Safari 26 on iOS uses a Liquid Glass toolbar and ignores the theme-color meta: it samples the CSS background and the backgrounds of fixed or sticky elements near the edges, even ones at opacity:0, so inactive overlays need display:none. viewport-fit=cover is required to extend under the bars, and 26.2 fixed fullscreen dialog backdrops. Home Screen apps on iOS can report a short innerHeight and 100dvh just after launch, so the app height should be re-measured.
- [medium] iOS platform limits: there is no screen.orientation.lock, no element Fullscreen API on iPhone (iPad only), and the manifest orientation field is not honoured. Android Chrome supports requestFullscreen, and orientation lock works only in fullscreen or an installed PWA. From iOS 26, any site added to the Home Screen opens as a standalone web app by default, even without a manifest. Screen Wake Lock shipped in Safari 16.4 but only works in Home Screen apps from iOS 18.4 (WebKit bug 254545).
- [medium,critical] File picking: a bare accept="audio/*" is unreliable on iOS and iPadOS. One iOS 17 report says it blocked picking any file, and another says MP3s in Files or iCloud appeared greyed out. The standard fix is to list explicit MIME types and extensions. Android reports .m4a variously as audio/mp4, audio/x-m4a or even video/mp4.
- [medium] PointerEvent.getCoalescedEvents() is not Baseline and needs a secure context, so scratch input must not depend on it. Feature-detect it and use it when present.
- [medium] Steven Hoober's 2013 study of how people hold phones: 49% one-handed, 36% cradled with the other hand tapping, and 15% two thumbs. For a DJ app expect two-handed or cradled use, so put frequent controls in the lower-middle and on the sides, not the top corners.
## Recommendations
- ORIENTATION STRATEGY: support portrait and landscape and never try to lock orientation (impossible on iOS). Phone portrait defaults to a 'Twin' layout: deck A on the LEFT and deck B on the RIGHT, matching crossfader direction. Stacked A/B zoomed waveforms always sit at the top and the crossfader is always visible at the bottom. The middle area is a swappable page (DECKS / MIX / FX / SONGS) chosen from a bottom tab bar. Phone landscape and larger screens use a 'Console' layout with everything except SONGS/FX visible at once. In the manifest set display:'standalone' and orientation:'any'.
- LAYOUT SELECTION (JS sets a class on <html> from innerWidth, innerHeight and matchMedia('(any-pointer:coarse)'), recomputed on resize, orientationchange and visualViewport resize): 'pp' (phone portrait) when width<600 and height>=width; 'pl' (phone landscape) when width>height and height<500; 'tp' (tablet portrait) when width>=600 and height>=width; 'console' (tablet landscape or desktop) when width>height and height>=500. Compute platter diameter in JS and expose it as --P. Do not compute it in CSS calc chains.
- GLOBAL TOKENS: --g (side gutter) = max(16px, env(safe-area-inset-left/right)). --tap = 48px on coarse pointers, 32px on fine. --tap-primary = 56px on phone, 64px on tablet. Minimum gap between adjacent targets is 4px when both are at least 48px, otherwise 8px. No drag-start zone within 16px of the left or right viewport edge (back-gesture zone). The crossfader track starts at least 44px from the screen edge.
- PHONE PORTRAIT 360x640 device (about 360x560 usable in the browser), content width 328 (360-2x16), two 160px columns with an 8px gap. Rows top to bottom: (1) header 44px = deck A chip 136 + gap 8 + menu button 40x44 + gap 8 + deck B chip 136. A chip shows an 'A'/'B' badge, the title at 12px/600 with ellipsis, and a second line with BPM at 15px/700 tabular and time remaining at 12px; tapping a chip opens the deck sheet (tempo range, vinyl mode, eject). (2) Waveform block 120px = A overview 16 + A zoomed 44 + B zoomed 44 + B overview 16, so the two zoomed waveforms touch with one shared 2px centre playhead. (3) Page area 1fr, about 272px. (4) Crossfader row 72px = 'A' 28 + track 272 (hit area 56 tall, visible line 6px) + 'B' 28, with a 40x56 thumb. (5) Bottom tabs 52px plus env(safe-area-inset-bottom): four tabs of 82x52 (icon 24 plus label 11px). DECKS page: platter 152px diameter centred in each column, gap 6, transport 52px tall (CUE 48w, PLAY 56w, SYNC 48w, 4px gaps = 160), gap 6, tempo row 48px (− 48w, BPM/% readout 56w, + 48w); total 264px.
- PHONE PORTRAIT MIX page (272px tall, 328 wide) = strip A 140 + centre 48 + strip B 140. Each strip has a 140x44 headphone PFL toggle on top. Below it: a 64px-wide column of FILTER, HI, MID and LOW controls (52-56px tall rows drawn as short vertical bar sliders, not tiny knobs), next to a 64px-wide channel volume fader with about 180px of travel. The centre 48px holds a master volume fader. Cue/master headphone mix and output devices go in the Outputs sheet. FX page: deck target [A|B] segmented control 44px tall; a 2x2 grid of FX pads (Echo, Reverb, Flanger, Crush), each 160x56; an FX amount slider 56px; beat-division chips (½ ¾ 1 2), each at least 48x44; and a one-shot row [ECHO OUT][BRAKE] 44px. SONGS page: '+ Add music' button 328x56; list rows 64px tall with title, artist and BPM, plus [A] and [B] load buttons of 48x48; the list alone gets touch-action:pan-y and overscroll-behavior:contain.
- PHONE PORTRAIT 390x844 device (about 390x660-700 usable in iOS Safari; measure it), content width 358, columns 175. Header 48, waveforms 2x(52+16)=136, crossfader 80, tabs 56. DECKS page about 344px: platter 168, transport 56 (CUE 52, PLAY 59, SYNC 52, 6px gaps), tempo row 52, plus a 48px quick row per deck (headphone PFL 48x48 and a 120px bipolar FILTER slider). In standalone mode (844 high, insets 47 top and 34 bottom, about 763 usable) grow the waveforms to 2x(64+18), the platter to 175 and the transport to 64.
- PHONE LANDSCAPE (e.g. 640x300 usable on Android Chrome; 844x390 iPhone, about 844x320-340 usable with left/right safe insets of about 47-62px and bottom 21px; measure, do not hard-code). Columns: [platter A column --P] [centre minmax(220px,1fr)] [platter B column --P], with --P = min(H-56-8, (W-2g-220-16)/2). Each platter column has the platter on top and the 56px transport row (CUE, PLAY, SYNC) below. The centre column has, top to bottom: stacked waveforms 2x(36-44 zoomed + 10-12 overview); a mixer of two strips (3 EQ bar sliders at least 44px each plus a 44-48px-wide volume fader about 132px tall); then a crossfader row 52-56px laid out as [headphone A 44][crossfader track][headphone B 44]. Put deck title and BPM as overlays in the waveform corners. FX and SONGS open from two 44px icon buttons as a sheet covering only the centre column, so the platters stay usable. Worked values: 844x340 gives a platter of about 255px with a 200px-wide centre; 640x300 gives about 194px with 220.
- TABLET PORTRAIT 768x1024 (about 768x950 usable in iPad Safari), gutter 20, content 728. Header 56: chips 340 each (title 15px, BPM 22px/700, key, time). Waveforms 2x(68 zoomed + 16 overview) = 168. Deck row 280 = vertical tempo fader A 56 + 8 + platter 264 + centre 64 (sync-link and phase indicator) + platter 264 + 8 + tempo fader B 56. Transport 72 (CUE 88x64, PLAY 112x64, SYNC 88x64). Mixer 200 = strip A 300 (FILTER/HI/MID/LOW as four 64x72 knobs in a row plus a 64x190 volume fader) + centre 128 (master volume, cue-mix knob, headphone A/B) + strip B 300. Crossfader 72 (track 400, thumb 48x64). Bottom tabs 48 (MIXER / FX / SONGS); FX and SONGS slide over the mixer row.
- TABLET LANDSCAPE and DESKTOP (console, at least 1024x700): max-width 1360px centred. Waveforms span the full width, 2x(88 zoomed + 20 overview). Columns are [deck A 1fr][mixer 360px][deck B 1fr]. Each deck has a vertical tempo fader (56 wide, about 240 travel), a platter of about 280, transport 64, and an FX block under the deck (4 pads, amount slider, optional XY pad 280x140). The mixer has 56px knobs, 200px faders and an 80px crossfader. A library panel along the bottom (about 30% height, resizable) is also a drag-and-drop target. Fine-pointer extras: mouse wheel adjusts knobs and faders (Shift for fine), double-click resets, hover tooltips, and keyboard shortcuts.
- FADERS (volume, tempo, crossfader, FX amount): use RELATIVE drag. Touching anywhere on the track grabs the current value with no jump, and value changes by dx/travelPx (or -dy/travelPx). A long-press of 450ms without movement switches to fine mode (x0.25). Double-tap the thumb or track to reset to its default (EQ 0 dB, tempo 0%, crossfader centre, volume 0.8). The crossfader has a ±2% centre detent with one haptic tick. Show a value bubble about 56px above the finger while dragging, because the finger hides the control. Volume uses a perceptual curve (e.g. gain = v^2 or a -60..0 dB taper); the crossfader defaults to constant-power, with a 'cut' curve option for scratching.
- KNOBS (EQ, filter, FX params): vertical drag with about 160-200px for the full range, where up means more; also accept horizontal movement (use dx - dy). The filter knob has a centre dead-zone of ±3-4% that snaps to off, with a haptic tick on entering it. EQ range is kill (-∞) to +6 dB with a detent at 0. On phones, draw them as bar sliders with the same gesture; on tablet and desktop draw them as knobs.
- BUTTONS THAT AFFECT TIMING (PLAY, CUE, SYNC, FX pads, hot buttons) fire on pointerdown, not click or pointerup, because click adds latency and fails if the finger moves slightly. CUE follows CDJ press/hold/release semantics. FX pads: a tap latches and a press longer than 300ms acts momentarily until release. Track active pointers per control by pointerId and call setPointerCapture so platter, fader and pad can be used with simultaneous fingers.
- PLATTER: when Vinyl mode is on (the default, since scratching was asked for), touching while playing holds the record: ramp to 0 over about 30-60ms, then follow the finger. Position change in seconds = (Δangle/2π) x 1.8 for 33⅓ rpm. Releasing ramps back to the tempo rate over about 120-200ms. Dragging while paused scrubs audibly. When Vinyl is off, angular velocity nudges the tempo: bend = clamp((ω/2π) x 6%, ±12%), decaying to 0 within 150ms of release. Use a separate rim NUDGE ring (30% of the radius, at least 28px) only when the platter is 200px or larger (landscape, tablet). On a 152px phone platter, put nudge on a waveform drag and on hold-to-bend −/+ buttons instead. Platter angle on screen = (playheadSeconds/1.8) x 360°, derived from the real playhead so rotation stays correct while scratching, reversing or changing tempo. Show a deck-colour glow ring while touched, a 4px elapsed-progress arc on the rim, a 12 o'clock marker stripe, and a flashing rim in the last 30s. Send target position plus a timestamp to the AudioWorklet and smooth there per sample (one-pole or alpha-beta, using Mixxx's alpha=1/8 and beta=alpha/32 as a starting point), because pointer events arrive at only 60-120 Hz.
- WAVEFORMS: both decks MUST use the same pixels-per-second so beats line up visually. Default visible window: 6s on phones (about 55 px/s on 328px), 8s on tablets, 10s on desktop; zoom range 2-20s via ± buttons or your own two-finger pinch on the waveform (browser zoom is disabled). Keep the playhead centred at 50%. Beat grid: bar lines 2px at full height and beat lines 1px at 40% height. Deck A's ticks grow up from its bottom edge and deck B's grow down from its top edge, so the two grids meet at the shared border and misalignment is obvious. Colour: 3-band within the deck's hue (low = saturated deck hue, mid = lighter tint, high = near-white) so deck identity survives, with options for 'RGB' (low red, mid green, high blue) and 'rekordbox 3-band' (blue/orange/white). Pre-render per-band peaks once to an offscreen canvas at a fixed px/s and drawImage with an offset each requestAnimationFrame, sized for devicePixelRatio. Dragging the zoomed waveform scrubs and sets cue while paused, and nudges while playing (djay-style).
- OVERVIEW STRIP (whole track, 16px tall, hit area at least 24px): dim the played part to 40%, show a cue-point triangle and a playhead line. Apply a Needle Lock: tap-to-seek works only when the deck is paused or inaudible (channel volume x crossfader gain below about -40 dB). While audible, seeking needs a 350ms press-and-hold, with a progress ring as feedback.
- SYNC: a tap matches tempo AND beat phase to the other deck (or the deck that is louder in the mix) and latches, lit in deck colour; while latched it follows the master's tempo changes. Tapping again unlatches and keeps the current tempo. Long-press resets tempo to 0% (the original BPM). Always show both BPMs, plus a small phase meter (6px bar, -½..+½ beat) between the stacked waveforms for very short heights.
- CUE vs HEADPHONES: label the cue-point button 'CUE' and the headphone pre-listen buttons with a headphone icon and 'PFL'/'🎧', never 'CUE'. Beginners confuse the two uses of the word. Default CUE uses CDJ semantics (above), and loading a track automatically sets cue on the first detected downbeat.
- LOAD FLOW: tapping an EMPTY platter opens the file picker for that deck directly. The SONGS page allows multi-select with A/B load buttons, and desktop accepts drag-and-drop onto a deck. Use <input type=file multiple accept="audio/*,.mp3,.wav,.m4a,.aac,.flac,.ogg,.oga,.opus,.aif,.aiff,.webm">. While decoding and analysing, show a progress ring on the platter with 'Analysing BPM…', and keep PLAY disabled until decoding finishes. Load Lock: loading onto a deck that is playing and audible needs a 600ms hold or a confirm sheet.
- BEGINNER MODE (default 'Easy', switchable to 'Pro' in settings). Easy: SYNC latches automatically when a track loads into the stopped deck; optional 'Tempo blend' crossfader (djay Starter-style) morphs the master tempo from A to B BPM as the crossfader moves; only 4 FX plus a per-channel FILTER; one-shot ECHO OUT (echo plus fade over 2 bars) and BRAKE (vinyl stop over 1 beat). Pro: tempo range ±8/16/50%, key lock, vinyl/nudge ring, cut crossfader curve. First-run onboarding is three coach marks ('Tap a record to load a song' → 'Press PLAY' → 'Load deck B, press SYNC, slide the crossfader'). Offer a 'Try with demo loops' button that renders two short procedural loops at 124 and 128 BPM with an OfflineAudioContext, so people can try it before picking files and no copyrighted demo audio is needed.
- FX UI: per deck, Echo (beat-synced ½/¾/1/2 beats, feedback 40%), Reverb, Flanger and Bitcrush as large pads, plus one FX amount (wet) control. On tablet and desktop add an XY pad (x = beat division or parameter, y = wet; touch-and-hold applies it and release stops it), matching Cross DJ and djay Starter. Filter stays on the mixer strip as the most-used 'effect'.
- VISUAL TOKENS: --bg #0B0D10, --surface #14171C, --raised #1C2027, --line #2A2F38, --text #F3F5F8, --text-2 #AEB6C2, --text-3 #7D8694 (keep at least 4.5:1 on --bg). Deck A #3AA0FF (blue) and deck B #FF8A1F (orange), a pair that stays distinct under red-green colour blindness; always also show 'A'/'B' letters. Active state = filled deck colour with #0B0D10 text and icon; inactive = 2px #3A404A outline with a #AEB6C2 icon. Danger/eject #FF4D5E. Provide a 'High contrast / outdoor' toggle that also follows prefers-contrast:more: pure #000 background, #FFF text, 3px strokes, +2px font sizes. Define light and dark tokens per the artifact contract, but default the booth UI to dark.
- TYPOGRAPHY: font stack system-ui, -apple-system, 'SF Pro Text', Roboto, 'Segoe UI', sans-serif. Every number (BPM, time, %, dB) uses font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums so readouts do not jitter. BPM 15-22px/700; time 12-15px/600; labels 11px/600 uppercase, letter-spacing 0.06-0.08em; never below 11px. Track titles get one line with ellipsis, and tapping shows the full title in the deck sheet.
- SAFE AREAS / PWA: viewport meta = width=device-width, initial-scale=1, viewport-fit=cover. #app is position:fixed with inset:0, height var(--app-h) set from window.innerHeight on resize and orientationchange and again 300ms after load (iOS standalone launch quirk), falling back to 100dvh. Pad it with env(safe-area-inset-*), using max(16px, env(...)) on the sides. Set body background-color to --bg because Safari 26 tints its toolbar from CSS. Hide inactive sheets with display:none, not opacity:0. Keep theme-color for Android and older Safari. Add apple-mobile-web-app-status-bar-style=black-translucent and a manifest with display standalone, background_color and theme_color #0B0D10, and icons. Request a Screen Wake Lock on first PLAY and re-request it on visibilitychange. Feature-detect requestFullscreen and offer a fullscreen button only on Android and desktop.
- HAPTICS helper: if navigator.vibrate exists (Android Chrome) call vibrate(8-12ms) for detents and 15-20ms for button presses, after the first user tap. Otherwise, on iOS 18+, click a hidden <label><input type=checkbox switch></label>. Use haptics only on discrete events (detent entered, PLAY/CUE/SYNC press, FX latch); never on continuous scratch or fader movement. Provide a Settings toggle for haptics. Treat the iOS hack as best-effort: it may not fire from pointermove or timer callbacks.
- GESTURE SUPPRESSION: on html and body set overflow:hidden and overscroll-behavior:none. On #app set touch-action:none, user-select:none, -webkit-user-select:none, -webkit-touch-callout:none and -webkit-tap-highlight-color:transparent. The SONGS list alone gets touch-action:pan-y and overscroll-behavior:contain. Call preventDefault on contextmenu within controls, and on gesturestart/gesturechange (non-passive listeners) for iOS. Handle pointercancel and lostpointercapture exactly like pointerup, keeping the last value. Never put interactive controls inside a scroll container.
- OUTPUT ROUTING UX (for the two-speaker requirement): an 'Outputs' sheet with MASTER → [device] and HEADPHONES → [device] pickers, a 'Ping' test-tone button next to each so the user can tell which is which, and a cue/master mix knob. Add a 'Split mono (L = master, R = headphones)' fallback, the same as djay's Split Output with a Y cable. When the platform cannot target two devices, say so plainly: djay documents that pre-cueing is not supported over Bluetooth/AirPlay on iOS. Choose the default from feature detection, not user-agent sniffing.
- IFRAME PREVIEW: inside a sandboxed or cross-origin iframe, pull-to-refresh and pinch belong to the top-level page, vibrate is blocked until the frame is activated, and Wake Lock and fullscreen need permissions-policy or allow attributes. Degrade all of these silently and test the real behaviour from the standalone URL.
## Gotchas
- iOS Safari ignores user-scalable=no. Without touch-action:none on every control and on its non-scrolling ancestors, two fingers on two controls can start a pinch-zoom, which fires pointercancel on both pointers.
- touch-action is not inherited, and changing it mid-gesture does nothing. Set it in CSS on the controls and the app root, not in JS during pointerdown.
- Firing PLAY or CUE on click (or pointerup) adds latency and drops presses where the finger slid a few pixels. Use pointerdown.
- Absolute-position faders jump the value on touch, causing a loud volume jump when a finger lands on the track. Use relative drag.
- Horizontal drags that start within about 16-24px of the screen edge trigger Android gesture-back or iOS Safari swipe-back. Web pages cannot use Android's Gesture Exclusion API.
- overscroll-behavior must be on BOTH html and body: Chrome Android honours it on body and Safari on html.
- iOS 26.1 reportedly ignores -webkit-touch-callout:none for text long-press menus, so do not render selectable text inside controls.
- No iOS browser supports navigator.vibrate (all use WebKit). The switch-input haptic hack needs iOS 18 or later and is not a spec feature, so it may break. Chrome Android vibrate returns false until the user has tapped the frame, including inside cross-origin iframes.
- Safari 26 ignores the theme-color meta and tints its toolbar from page CSS, including fixed elements at opacity:0. Use display:none for hidden overlays and set an explicit body background.
- iOS standalone Home Screen apps can report a too-short innerHeight or 100dvh right after launch. Re-measure after load, on resize and on orientationchange.
- There is no orientation lock and no element fullscreen on iPhone, and the manifest orientation field is ignored on iOS. The layout must work in both orientations.
- Screen Wake Lock in iOS Home Screen web apps only works from iOS 18.4. Earlier, from 16.4, it worked only in Safari tabs.
- accept="audio/*" alone can grey out or block MP3s in the iOS file picker. List the extensions explicitly.
- djay's own docs say pre-cueing over Bluetooth/AirPlay is unsupported on iOS, so do not promise dual-Bluetooth routing in the UI without runtime detection.
- The word CUE means both 'cue point' and 'headphone pre-listen'. Using it for both confuses beginners, so use a headphone icon for PFL.
- If the two waveforms use different pixels-per-second, visual beatmatching is impossible. Lock both decks to one zoom.
- A nudge ring on a 150px platter is only about 17px wide, too narrow for a finger. Use a whole-platter mode toggle plus waveform-drag nudge on phones.
- Driving platter rotation from a timer drifts from the audio. Derive the angle from the playhead position, which is reported from the worklet.
- getCoalescedEvents is not Baseline. Feature-detect it and smooth scratch input in the worklet instead of relying on dense pointer samples.
- Bluetooth outputs add roughly 150-300ms of latency, and the amount differs between a speaker and headphones (my estimate; not verified in this research). The headphone cue may sound early or late relative to the master, which affects beatmatching by ear. Plan a per-output latency offset.

## Open questions
- Does the iOS 18+ switch-input haptic fire when label.click() is called from pointermove, from pointerdown on touch (which may not count as user activation), or from timers? Is it rate-limited? Test on a real device before relying on it for detents.
- What are the exact usable viewport heights in iOS 26 Safari, portrait and landscape, with the floating Liquid Glass tab bar? Is the layout viewport extended under the bar? The 360x560 and 390x660 budgets here are estimates; measure innerHeight and the safe-area insets on real devices.
- Can the web platform route two outputs at once (AudioContext.setSinkId on Chrome Android with two Bluetooth sinks, nothing equivalent on iOS Safari)? This needs the audio-routing research. The UX here assumes a split-mono fallback.
- I could not confirm djay's exact iPhone portrait 2-deck layout (the manual PDFs could not be text-extracted) or edjing's current phone layout. Verify against screenshots before copying a specific arrangement.
- Does a CDJ-style track-end warning (flashing jog ring, conventionally 30s) match current CDJ defaults? I did not verify it; treat it as a design choice.
- Does iOS Safari now support PointerEvent.getCoalescedEvents? The MDN compat table was not readable; feature-detect it.
- Is a Bluetooth latency-offset control for the headphone cue needed? That depends on measured output latency per device (AudioContext.outputLatency support differs by browser).
